The deal, in plain terms

  • Your files are never touched. The node works inside its own folder. It cannot see the rest of your machine, and the website never sees your files.
  • A fixed job menu. The site can ask for named jobs only. It can never send your machine new commands or arbitrary code.
  • Your caps, your call. You set disk, hours, and load limits. The kill switch sits on your box, not ours — leaving is one click, and it removes everything.
  • Results drain to the Factory floor. Your box keeps no record it's responsible for; finished work moves to the floor and is verified there.
  • Nothing is trusted on its word. Every result is checked by hash before it counts.
  • Nodes are handles, not people. The network knows your machine by a node name — not your name, not your address.

Enrollment is by invite. Your invite link downloads the installer with your connect step built in — run it, enter your code when it prompts you to connect to the website, and you're a node. No invite yet? If you run a machine that should be a floor, get in touch through the site and say so.

What does it cost me?
Electricity and spare cycles you're not using. Jobs are capped, pausable, and scheduled around you — a node should never make your machine hot, loud, or slow when you need it.
What does my box actually do?
It fetches public data — government releases, public records, open datasets — and processes it: cleaning, keying, and reconciling it into the substrates the Factory's tools and trackers run on. The same work the Factory floor does today, spread across the network.
Can I leave?
Any time. One click stops the node and removes it. No notice, no penalty, no hard feelings.
